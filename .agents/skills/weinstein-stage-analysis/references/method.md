# Method and decision distinctions

Source identifiers refer to [sources.md](sources.md). These are qualitative analytical rules. Numerical thresholds, bar conventions and automatic eligibility gates require the separate [evidence contract](evidence.md).

## Market, group and instrument

Evaluate the broad environment before choosing the strongest groups and individual candidates. Record agreement or conflict across these levels; an attractive isolated chart does not erase a hostile environment. Relative performance is comparison with a benchmark, not RSI. [B1; S1; S2]

Use breadth, advance/decline behavior and market trend as separate observations when available. Do not invent a combined “Weinstein market score.” Historical indicators are covered by the book, but exact formulas and signal thresholds beyond this skill's verified scope must be sourced before use. [B1, chapter 8; local reporting design]

## Stages describe context

| Context | Evidence to examine | Decision implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stage 1: base | Prior decline slows; a range develops and the long average levels out | Watch for a credible transition, not an automatic purchase |
| Stage 2: advance | Upward escape from a base or continuation structure, sustained strength and eventually a rising long average | Consider an entry only at a suitable setup |
| Stage 3: topping/distribution | A preceding advance loses progress; range and flatter average develop | Review exposure and protection; not every pause proves distribution |
| Stage 4: decline | Support gives way, weakness persists and the long average falls | Avoid treating cheapness alone as a long setup; consider bearish analysis |

Interpret these jointly. Similar sideways prices after a decline and after an advance have different context. Missing prior history warrants uncertainty. A rebound within a decline need not be a new Stage 2. [S1; S2; local uncertainty handling]

## Long entries and mode

**Investor:** initial base breakout or a suitable first retest. The long average need not already be rising; no longer declining is a material distinction. **Trader:** continuation within an established advance requires rising-average context. Do not switch modes retrospectively to justify a losing position. [B1, glossary and chapter 3; S1]

Inspect the actual resistance boundary and overhead supply, not just the average. Separate a genuine breakout from an intraperiod excursion. A retest requires evidence that the former boundary is behaving as support; a cheap price alone does not establish one. [S2; local bar convention]

Breakout participation matters. The book describes a weekly surge relative to the prior month and a multiweek accumulation alternative. A single universal ratio loses that distinction. Improving relative strength can remain below zero; do not automatically reject it solely for its sign. [B1, chapter 4]

Treat an extended advance as an entry-timing problem even when the stage is clear. Explain whether a new consolidation/retest would change eligibility; never invent the future trigger price when the chart levels are absent. [S2; local reporting design]

## Position management

Keep the investment horizon and the trading horizon explicit when discussing stops and exits. Review structural support, the existing protective level and the sequence since entry. Avoid loosening protection merely to preserve a preferred stage interpretation. Topping evidence can call for a management review before a full declining phase. [S3]

For a supplied stop breach, identify the breach, time and source, then discuss exit/protection action without waiting for a slower classification. If no position or stop is known, request the missing facts or give conditional analysis; never invent the prior stop. Execution fill and stop-order mechanics are outside this skill. [Local decision boundary]

When translating a correction/recovery sequence into code, preserve event order: prior high, correction, recovery, then any eligible trailing adjustment. Entry return is not drawdown from a subsequent peak. This is an implementation safeguard, not a claim that one percentage captures every book stop rule. [Local adaptation]

## Short analysis

Assess a top, support breakdown or failed rally together with market/group weakness and relative underperformance. Avoid initiating solely because price has fallen far. Treat rally context and protective resistance as part of the setup. Downside volume need not mirror the strong upside breakout-volume requirement. [S3; B1, chapter 7]

A bearish setup can be analyzed even when the host is long-only; label it analytically eligible but execution unavailable. Borrow availability, costs, liquidation and order permissions need separate current checks before any implementation. [Local host boundary]

## Coverage boundary

The core workflow covers stages, selection, common entries, position review and short analysis. Special aggressive-entry patterns, exact market-indicator implementations, and funds/options/futures applications are research extensions. Consult the source register and retrieve supporting material before answering specific rules in those areas. Do not invent “triple confirmation” as any three favorable indicators or claim the book's full contents are encoded here.
